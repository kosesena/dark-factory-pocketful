## Finding 1: importing genuine stage-3 exports with legacy snapshots exceeds the 5 s per-request limit
- **Requirements broken:** the run contract ("5s per request"), together with stage 4 "A stage-4 service must accept exports produced by the same team's stages 1–3, retaining ... snapshots". The coordinator's handoff also asks that "more than 2000 later facts still import genuine legacy snapshots".
- **Evidence:**
  - Source: a real 495d5d6 export with 5 metadata-free snapshots taken just after reset, then 2100 later payments.
  - Imported into the stage-4 container limited to 2 vCPU and 2 GiB:

    | snapshots in export | import time (3 runs) |
    |---|---|
    | 0 | 0.07–0.13 s |
    | 1 | 1.23, 1.23, 1.24 s |
    | 4 | 4.11, 4.38, 4.33 s |
    | 5 | **5.25, 5.16, 5.16 s** |
    | 10 | **10.17 s** |

  - `verification/probes_legacy_migration.py http://localhost:9524 http://localhost:9533` fails `A 2100 later facts, 5 legacy snapshots: import within 5 s -> 5.34s`; reruns gave 5.63 s, and 5.14 s on the uncapped container.
  - The import does return 204 and keeps the original pages; only the time limit is broken.
- **Cause, read in the diff:** for each legacy snapshot, `load_state` walks every recorded moment from the latest back to the snapshot's floor, and calls `build_statement` over the whole ledger at each moment. That is about 1 s per snapshot per 2100 later facts, and the cost grows with facts × moments. `_past_moments` itself also runs an O(times × facts) check.
- **Smallest reproduction:**
  1. On 495d5d6: reset with u_a and u_b, then log in as u_a.
  2. Take 5 statements, `GET /statement?limit=1` (any windows).
  3. Make 2100 payments of 1 from a to b.
  4. `GET /_test/export`, then time `POST /_test/import` of that export on stage 4. It takes more than 5 s.
  5. With the snapshots removed from the export, the same import takes 0.1 s.

## Ruling challenge (information, not a reject reason)
- The two ruled classes do not exhaust what the earlier-moment search admits. **Reverting a later correction** inside a stripped snapshot also imports (204). Example: the entry for p_s1 goes back from revision 2 / 250 to revision 1 / 300, with balances and closing adjusted. The paged entry then shows revision 1 / 300 where the genuine page showed revision 2 / 250 (probe section B, printed as INFO).
- This tampered snapshot is byte-identical to a genuine metadata-free snapshot taken before that correction. Section A "later correction" migrates exactly that state and must give 204. So stage 4 cannot refuse it without refusing genuine exports.
- I recommend widening the ruling to "the statement as of any earlier recorded moment not before the snapshot's own latest entry". That covers drop-last, empty-ledger and revert-later-correction.
- No other tamper I tried got through.
- The cross-auditor's `AUDIT_LEGACY_ALTERNATIVES=1` probe (1578e5e) expects 204 for the cleared-entries case on its seeded-only fixture, and stage 4 gives 422. That ledger has no recorded moment before the seeded payments, so the refusal is correct: §10 is not violated, and the class is admitted, not required.
